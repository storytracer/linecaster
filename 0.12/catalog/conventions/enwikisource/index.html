Source¶

English Wikisource (en.wikisource.org), its Page: pages as their wikitext is read by the MediaWiki importer: expanded with the wiki's own templates, Lua modules and style sheets from the dump of 2026-11-01, built into HTML. The rules match the classes and attributes the templates write; they extend mediawiki, which holds what MediaWiki and its extensions write on every wiki.

How the markup works¶

English Wikisource's editors transcribe a scanned page into its Page: page: the running head and anything printed above the text in the page's header, the text in its body, a footer below. They mark what the page prints with templates whose output is HTML with classes of their own (wst-*, ws-*) and TemplateStyles style sheets: the running head's left, centre and right ({{RunningHeader}}, {{rh}}), centred and indented blocks, poems ({{ppoem}}), sidenotes, ditto marks, dot leaders in tables of contents, figures ({{FreedImg}}), corrections of printing errors ({{SIC}}) and notes of the editors ({{tooltip}}, {{Errata}}). Most of it is read by HTML's own semantics: blocks and inline elements, visibility: hidden ({{dhr}}, {{gap}}), text-transform in the style sheets ({{uc}}, an Index's styles), ARIA headings, lang. The rules say the rest: what a class means where HTML does not.

A page's last paragraph runs on onto the next page unless the page ends it: {{nop}} marks the end of a paragraph at a page's end.

  • Stanzas across a page break (editions 2 and 3): {{ppoem}} writes a complete poem on each Page: page, and start=follow / end=follow join a stanza only in the main namespace. The reader records each top-level template call on the first element it writes (data-mw-template, data-mw-param-<name>), so the rules match the call: a page's first stanza continues (on the poem's element), its last runs_on (on its stanzas: one element takes one decision, and a call can have both). Each page reads as it is; an item joins them.
  • Words divided across a page break (edition 4): {{hws}} and {{hwe}} ({{hyphenated word start}}, {{hyphenated word end}}) write each half as printed, in a span whose title is the whole word; the main namespace prints the whole word where {{hwe}} stands and nothing for {{hws}}. Each half is read as printed with a joined edit giving the whole word; an item's pages read together (link_pages) pair the halves and write the whole word, including a compound's own hyphen (inter-crossing), where the wiki does. The reader marks a call inside a line too when a rule names its template, so the rule matches the span the call writes.

The transcription itself is already normalized, and the entry says so (transcription, copied into each page's Source.normalized; scoring checks a lane against it): the long s is written s and typographic ligatures (ﬁ, ﬂ, ﬀ, ﬅ) as their letters, æ and œ kept, r rotunda modernized (Wikisource:Style guide/Orthography); a word broken over two lines is rejoined, a true hyphen kept (Help:Beginner's guide to typography); MediaWiki stores the text in NFC.

Not in the rules¶

  • Templates whose output has no class of its own and that no rule names cannot be matched in the HTML: {{ua}} (an editor's note, its text followed by "(Wikisource contributor note)"). Its text is read as printed text.
  • A word divided across two pages is joined only where an item's pages are read together, not on one page.
  • What {{FreedImg}} and a file link show is a picture region with the file's name; the importer has no image.

Open questions¶

  • {{ua}}: a rule naming the template ([data-mw-template=Ua]) would match its output as {{hws}}'s is.
  • Dot leaders (ws-noexport) are removed whole; a list of illustrations that sets credits inside the leader element loses them.

Rules¶

Rule Match Decision Why
running-head-cells {'css': '.wst-rh-cell'} cell {{RunningHeader}} ({{rh}}, {{rvh}} and their variants) prints the running head's left, centre and right as one line: its cells are one region of the page's furniture, each cell a region of its own.
no-run-on {'css': '.wst-nop'} break {{nop}} ends the paragraph: at the end of a page it says that the page's last paragraph does not run on onto the next page.
corrections {'css': ".wst-tooltip[title^='[sic]']"} edit {'kind': 'corrected', 'attr': 'title', 'extract': "^\\[sic\\]\\s*'(.*)'$"} {{SIC}} shows the text as printed and the editors' correction in its tooltip ("[sic] 'the'"): the printed text is the page's, the correction an edit of it.
tooltips {'css': '.wst-tooltip'} describe {'kind': 'note', 'attr': 'title'} {{tooltip}} shows a note of the editors when the pointer rests on the text; the note is not printed.
sidenotes {'css': '.wst-sidenote'} note {'role': 'marginalia'} {{left sidenote}} and {{right sidenote}} print a note in the margin beside the paragraph where they stand: a region of its own (marginalia), not part of the paragraph's text.
ditto-hidden {'css': '.__ditto_hidden'} invisible {{ditto}} prints a ditto mark where a word is repeated; the word itself is kept, invisible, to give the mark its width.
dot-leaders {'css': '.ws-noexport'} remove {'reason': 'generated'} Elements of class ws-noexport (dot leaders of tables of contents, navigation) are drawn by the templates and left out of exports by WS Export; the page prints leaders, not text.
figure-captions {'css': '.wst-freedimg-caption'} caption {{FreedImg}} prints a figure's caption under or over it.
figure-boxes {'css': 'span.wst-freedimg'} text_block {'role': 'picture'} {{FreedImg/span}} sets a figure in spans; its box is a block of its own, as the figure is on the page.
errata-notes {'css': 'aside[data-group=errata]'} remove {'reason': 'editorial', 'keep_text': True} {{Errata}} shows the printed text and adds the editors' correction as a note of the group errata; the note is the editors', not the page's.
stanza-continued {'css': '[data-mw-template=Ppoem][data-mw-param-start=follow]'} continues {{ppoem|start=follow}}: the page's first stanza continues the previous page's last; the module writes a complete poem on the Page: page, so only the call says it (the reader's data-mw-template and data-mw-param-start).
stanza-runs-on {'css': '.ws-poem-stanza', 'inside': '[data-mw-template=Ppoem][data-mw-param-end=follow]'} runs_on {{ppoem|end=follow}}: the page's last stanza runs on into the next page's first. Matched on the poem's stanzas, since a call with start=follow too has its poem's element decided by stanza-continued.
divided-words {'css': "span[data-mw-template=Hws], span[data-mw-template=Hwe], span[data-mw-template='Hyphenated word start'], span[data-mw-template='Hyphenated word end']"} edit {'kind': 'joined', 'attr': 'title'} {{hws}} and {{hwe}} write each half of a word divided across a page break as printed, the whole word in the span's title ("de-", "pends": "depends"); in the main namespace the wiki prints the whole word in its place. Each half is the page's text, the whole word a joined edit of it; an item joins the two (linecaster-mediawiki's link_pages). Matched by the call (the reader's data-mw-template), since the span has no class of its own.
